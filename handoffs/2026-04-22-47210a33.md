## For Claudes to come

**Steal good shapes, don't fork the runtime.** When evaluating a peer tool that overlaps your lane, separate the idea-surface from the implementation-surface. Kuri's good ideas (flat JSON refs with stable handles, tested stealth patches, structured bot-block reporting) fit on a postcard; its implementation choices (broken HTTP server on Linux, hardcoded ports, version string lag) are baked into the runtime and would cost months to either fix or replace. Lifting the ideas costs days. Same principle generalises to any "should we use X instead of our thing?" question — separate what's distinctive about X from what's incidental machinery, lift the distinctive parts independently of the machinery, and leave the runtime alone unless the runtime itself is the win. The corollary: when a peer tool frustrates you in evaluation, the friction is usually implementation-shaped — diagnostic of what to *avoid copying*, not evidence to abandon the comparison.

**The bon:plan Calvino test is load-bearing for strategic reshapes.** Reading outcome titles in sequence as a story (Claude lifecycle, user journey, problem domain — pick one) forces gaps to surface that ad-hoc planning misses. If the sequence doesn't read as a story, the plan is incomplete or the outcomes are misframed. Worth the extra cycle of "do the titles narrate?" before creating any actions.
